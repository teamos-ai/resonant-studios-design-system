  Top Customer Problems

L ist the biggest pain points your ideal customer is facing right now.
1. Lack of Safe, Accessible Creative Outlets
N DIS participants often lack spaces where they can express themselves through music without
judgment or barriers. Mainstream studios are physically inaccessible, intimidating, and staffed by
p eople who don't understand disability or communication needs. This leaves participants isolated,
u nder-stimulated, and without meaningful ways to build confidence or develop creative skills.

2 . No Tangible, Shareable Outcomes from Creative Activities
Many community-based creative programs focus on "participation" but fail to deliver something
participants can be proud of and share with family and friends. Participants want more than just
"having fun"--they want to create something real: a finished song, a recording on Spotify, proof of
their ability and growth[3][4]. Without tangible outcomes, activities feel temporary and don't build
lasting confidence or a sense of achievement.

3. Rigid, One-Size-Fits-All Programs That Don't Adapt
Most NDIS-funded creative programs follow fixed curriculums that ignore individual goals,
c apacity, and interests. Participants with varying skill levels, support needs, and creative
a mbitions are forced into group structures that don't accommodate their pace or
preferences[5][6]. This leads to frustration, disengagement, and participants abandoning
p rograms that could otherwise be life- changing.

1. Unregistered NDIS Provider Status Limits Market Access
No NDIS Registration + Agency-Managed Plans = Reduced Addressable Market

 Threat Analysis (SWOT)

What external risks could harm your business or slow your growth?

1. NDIS Policy Changes or Funding Cuts to Creative Supports
Price Cap Changes + Eligibility Tightening = Revenue Risk
NDIS pricing arrangements and category eligibility are subject to government review, with recent
changes to art and music therapy funding demonstrating policy volatility[31][32]. If community
participation line items face price reductions or stricter eligibility criteria, Resonant Studios's
revenue model and participant access could be threatened. Continuous monitoring of NDIS policy
and diversification into private-pay options may be necessary.

2. Established Music Therapy Providers Expanding into Non-Therapy Space
Registered Providers + Existing Networks = Competitive Pressure
Established, registered music therapy providers with strong support coordinator networks could
expand into the non-therapeutic, creative studio space, leveraging their existing credibility and
referral relationships[33][34]. If they offer similar outcomes (songwriting, recording) without the
"therapy" label, they could quickly capture market share. Resonant Studios's competitive moat
d epends on maintaining superior participant experience, faster outcomes, and stronger NDIS
literacy.
3.Economic Downturn Reducing NDIS Plan Sizes
Smaller Plans + Budget Pressure = Reduced Discretionary Spending
In economic downturns or periods of NDIS budget constraints, participants may receive smaller
p lans with less funding allocated to discretionary supports like creative activities[35]. Support
coordinators may prioritise essential daily living supports over music sessions, reducing demand.
Building strong outcome documentation and positioning music as a capacity-building investment
(not luxury) will be critical to maintaining referrals.
